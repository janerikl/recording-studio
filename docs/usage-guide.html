A feature-by-feature guide to Recording Studio, in the order you'd naturally encounter them: starting a project, playing and recording audio, editing clips, mixing, adding effects, and exporting your work.

Project Basics

Starting a project

When you launch the app, you get one blank audio track ready to record — no setup needed.

How: just open the app. If "Load Most Recent Session on Startup" is checked (File menu), your last project reopens instead.

Save Session

Saves your whole project — every track, clip, effect, and automation curve — to a project file.

How: File → Save Session, or Ctrl+S. The first save asks where to put the file; after that it saves to the same place automatically.

Load Session

Opens a previously saved project.

How: File → Load Session, then pick a .rsdproj file.

Open Recent

Quick access to your last 5 projects without browsing for the file.

How: File → Open Recent → pick from the list.

Load Most Recent Session on Startup

Skips the blank-project screen and reopens your last project automatically when you launch the app.

How: File menu → check "Load Most Recent Session on Startup".

Close Session

Closes the current project and clears the workspace.

How: File → Close Session.

Transport & Playback

Play

Resumes playback from wherever the playhead currently is.

How: Toolbar Play button, or Space.

Play from Start

Jumps to the very beginning of the project and starts playing.

How: Toolbar button, or Shift+Space.

Stop

Stops playback or recording. If you were recording, it finalizes whatever you captured as a new clip.

How: Toolbar Stop button, or Space while playing.

Seek by clicking the ruler

Jump the playhead instantly to any point in time.

How: Click anywhere on the time ruler at the top of the timeline.

Scrub by dragging the ruler

Drag through the timeline while hearing audio continuously, like scrubbing tape.

How: Left-click and drag along the ruler.

Recording

Record

Captures audio into whichever tracks are armed.

How: Arm a track (Rec/Arm checkbox on its mixer strip), then press R or the Record button. Press Stop or Space when done.

Multi-source recording

Record your microphone and your computer's audio (e.g. music, a call) at the same time onto separate tracks.

How: Arm one track set to "Mic" and another set to "System Audio" on their mixer strips, then Record as normal.

Choosing an input source per track

Each track can record from a microphone or from your system's audio output — set independently per track.

How: On the track's mixer strip, use the source dropdown (Mic / Sys).

Punch / Loop Recording

Punch region

Restrict recording to just a section of the timeline, so you only re-record the part that needs fixing.

How: Right-click-drag on the ruler to draw the region, or type exact start/end times into the In/Out fields on the toolbar.

Loop Record

Repeatedly records over the punch region on every pass (with a short pre-roll before each pass), so you can try a part multiple times without stopping.

How: Check "Loop Record" on the toolbar, define a punch region, arm a track, then press Record. Press Stop to end the loop. Every pass is kept as a "take" you can pick between later (see Recording Takes).

Clip Editing

Select a clip

Click a clip to select it before editing, deleting, or moving it.

How: Click the clip in its track's lane.

Move a clip

Reposition a clip in time, or move it to a different track.

How: Drag the middle of the clip (not its edges) left/right, or into another track's lane.

Trim start / end

Shorten a clip from either end without deleting it entirely.

How: Drag the left or right edge of the clip.

Fade in / fade out

Smoothly ramp a clip's volume up at the start or down at the end, instead of a hard cut.

How: Drag from the top-left corner of a clip for a fade-in, or the top-right corner for a fade-out.

Adjust clip gain

Change the volume of a single clip, independent of the track's overall volume.

How: Drag the horizontal line running through the middle of the clip up or down.

Split a clip

Cut a clip into two separate pieces at a specific point — handy for removing an unwanted section.

How: Position the playhead where you want the cut, then double-click the clip.

Delete a clip

Removes the selected clip from the track entirely.

How: Select the clip, then press Delete or Backspace.

Track Management

Add Audio Track

Adds a new track for recording or holding imported audio.

How: Toolbar "Add Track" button.

Add Instrument Track

Adds a track with a built-in synthesizer you can play via an on-screen keyboard and record as MIDI.

How: Toolbar "Add Instrument Track" button.

Add Bus Track

Adds an auxiliary bus other tracks can send audio to — useful for grouping tracks under a shared effect like reverb.

How: Toolbar "Add Bus Track" button.

Remove Track

Deletes the selected track and all its clips.

How: Select the track, then click "Remove Track".

Select (activate) a track

Marks a track as the active one — new imports and effect edits target it.

How: Click the "Active" radio button on the track's header, or press Ctrl+Alt+1 through Ctrl+Alt+9 for tracks 1–9.

Rename a track

Give a track a more descriptive name than "Track 1".

How: Double-click the track name and type a new one.

Mixer Controls

Mixer panel

A dedicated view showing every track as a vertical channel strip, side by side — like a hardware mixing console.

How: View menu → Mixer, to show/hide the docked panel at the bottom of the window.

Mute

Silences a track so you can hear the rest of the mix without it.

How: Mute checkbox on the track's mixer strip.

Solo

Mutes every other track so you can hear this one in isolation.

How: Solo checkbox on the track's mixer strip.

Arm for recording

Marks a track to receive audio the next time you press Record.

How: Arm/Rec checkbox on the mixer strip.

Volume

Sets how loud a track plays in the overall mix.

How: Vertical fader on the track's mixer strip.

Pan

Positions a track's sound between the left and right speakers.

How: Pan dial on the track's mixer strip.

Peak meter

Shows a track's real-time signal level, so you can spot clipping.

How: Watch the vertical meter bar next to each mixer strip's fader.

Sends & Bus Routing

Send to a bus

Routes a copy of a track's audio to a bus track (for example, sending several vocal tracks to one shared reverb bus).

How: On the track's mixer strip, choose a destination in the send-bus dropdown and set the send level.

Master volume

Controls the overall output level of the entire mix.

How: Master volume fader on the toolbar or mixer panel.

Effects

Add an effect

Inserts an EQ, Compressor, Delay, or Reverb into a track's (or the master bus's) signal chain.

How: Click the "FX" button on a mixer strip (or "Master FX" for the overall mix), then choose an effect type to add.

Adjust effect parameters

Fine-tune how an effect sounds.

How: Open the FX popover — each added effect shows its controls immediately, no separate dialog needed.

Bypass an effect

Temporarily disables an effect without deleting it, so you can A/B compare with and without it.

How: Toggle the checkbox next to the effect's name in the FX popover.

Reorder effects

Changes the order effects are applied in, which changes the resulting sound.

How: Drag an effect up or down within the FX popover.

Remove an effect

Deletes an effect from the chain.

How: Click the remove icon next to the effect in the FX popover.

3-Band EQ

Boosts or cuts low, mid, and high frequencies to shape a track's tone.

How: Add "EQ", then adjust the Low/Mid/High gain and frequency controls.

Compressor

Evens out volume by reducing loud peaks, so quiet and loud parts sit closer together.

How: Add "Compressor", then set Threshold, Ratio, Attack, Release, and Makeup Gain.

Delay

Adds an echo effect that repeats the sound after a set amount of time.

How: Add "Delay", then set Delay Time, Feedback, and Mix.

Reverb

Simulates the sound of a physical space (room, hall) around the track.

How: Add "Reverb", then set Room Size, Damping, and Mix.

Automation

Automation lane

Lets a track's volume, pan, or send level change over time automatically instead of staying fixed — for example, fading a track out gradually.

How: Click the "Auto" toggle on a track row to reveal its automation lane, then choose Volume, Pan, or Send Level as the target.

Add / move / delete automation points

Shapes the automation curve by placing control points at specific times and values.

How: Click on the automation lane to add a point, drag a point to reshape the curve, or select a point and press Delete to remove it.

MIDI / Instrument Editing

Piano Roll

A grid for viewing and editing the MIDI notes on an Instrument track.

How: Select an Instrument track, then open the Piano Roll panel (View menu, if hidden).

Draw, move, resize, delete notes

Directly edit a MIDI performance without re-recording it.

How: Click-drag in an empty area to draw a new note; drag an existing note to move it; drag its right edge to resize; select and press Delete to remove it.

Adjust note velocity

Controls how hard each note is "struck," which affects its loudness/tone from the synth.

How: Drag in the velocity lane beneath the main piano-roll grid.

Snap to grid

Aligns notes to musical beat subdivisions so they land precisely on the beat.

How: Choose a snap setting (off, 1/4, 1/8, 1/16, 1/32) from the Piano Roll panel's dropdown.

On-screen keyboard & synth controls

Play and shape the built-in synthesizer's sound — waveform shape, ADSR envelope, and filter cutoff.

How: Select an Instrument track, then use the on-screen keyboard and the Instrument panel's Waveform / ADSR / Filter controls.

Record MIDI

Captures notes you play on the on-screen keyboard as an editable MIDI performance.

How: Select and arm an Instrument track, press Record, play notes on the on-screen keyboard, then Stop.

Recording Takes

Take lanes

Every pass of a loop recording is kept as a separate "take," so you can compare them and pick the best one.

How: Click the "Takes" toggle on a track row to reveal all takes stacked below the main clip lane.

Promote a take

Makes a specific take the one that actually plays back, replacing whatever was there before.

How: Click the take lane you want to use.

Waveform Display

Track & master waveforms

Visualizes audio as a waveform so you can see where sound is loud, quiet, or silent — useful for aligning edits by eye.

How: Waveforms draw automatically in each track's lane; the strip below the ruler shows the combined mix of all audible tracks.

Playhead indicator

A vertical line showing exactly where playback or recording currently is.

How: Always visible; moves automatically during playback/recording, or jump it by clicking the ruler.

Media Management

Media Library

Lists every audio file used in the session, so you can drag it onto a track again without re-importing.

How: Open via the right-side panel tabs; drag an item onto a track lane to place it.

Import Audio File

Brings an external audio file (WAV, FLAC, OGG, AIFF) into the active track.

How: File → Import, then choose a file.

Drag & drop from your file manager

Import a file just by dragging it from your desktop's file browser onto a track.

How: Drag the file onto a track lane; it's placed at the drop position.

Loop Browser

Browse a sample folder

Lets you audition audio files from any folder on disk (like a sample pack) before adding them to your project.

How: Open the Loop Browser panel tab, click "Choose Folder", and pick a directory.

Filter, preview, and drop loops

Quickly find and try a sound before committing it to a track.

How: Type in the filter box to narrow the list; click a file to preview it (click again or hit Stop to stop); drag it onto a track to place it.

Import / Export

Export mixdown

Renders your whole project down to a single finished audio file.

How: File → Export, choose 32-bit float or 16-bit PCM WAV, then pick where to save.

Export stems

Also saves each track as its own separate audio file alongside the full mixdown — useful for sending to a collaborator or another studio.

How: In the Export dialog, check "Also export stems" before exporting.

Zoom & Navigation

Zoom in / out / reset

Controls how much timeline detail is visible — zoom in to edit precisely, zoom out to see the whole project.

How: Toolbar zoom buttons, Ctrl+= / Ctrl+-, or hold Ctrl and scroll over the timeline.

Scrolling

Move around the timeline when zoomed in, or scroll to see more tracks.

How: Use each track's scrollbar, or hold Shift and scroll to move all tracks together.

Bookmarks

Set a numbered bookmark

Marks a position in time so you can jump straight back to it later — great for marking a verse, chorus, or a spot that needs fixing.

How: Position the playhead, then press Ctrl+Shift+1 through Ctrl+Shift+9.

Jump to a bookmark

Instantly moves the playhead to a saved bookmark.

How: Press Ctrl+1 through Ctrl+9.

Playback Loop

Loop a section during playback

Repeats a section of the timeline continuously during playback, so you can listen to a passage over and over without restarting it manually.

How: Hold Ctrl and drag on the ruler to define the loop region. Hold Ctrl and click (without dragging) to turn the loop off again.

Settings

Audio Settings

Choose which speaker/interface plays audio, which microphone records, which system-audio source captures desktop sound, and the sample rate.

How: File → Settings.

Undo & Redo

Undo / Redo

Reverts or reapplies your last action — recordings, edits, effect changes, track add/remove, and more. Up to 50 steps are kept.

How: Ctrl+Z to undo, Ctrl+Y to redo.

View & Panels

Show/hide panels

Toggle which panels (Media Library, Instrument, Piano Roll, Loop Browser, Mixer) are visible, so you can focus on what you're working on.

How: View menu → check/uncheck a panel.

Window layout is remembered

Your window size, position, and panel arrangement are saved automatically and restored the next time you open the app.

How: Nothing to do — it's automatic.

Status & Meters

Status label

Shows whether you're stopped, playing, or recording, plus a quick summary of the session.

How: Read it at the top of the window — updates automatically.

Input / output meters

Real-time level meters for your microphone input and the final mixed output, so you can watch for clipping.

How: Always visible at the top of the window.
